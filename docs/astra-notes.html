What Astra's notes explain

For the picture lessons, start with the learning guide. This page lets a helper check what the published notes actually support.

Reviewed 5 October 2026. These are public-game runs. The best provider-adapter semi-private result was high effort. The earlier guide covered max; it was not a review of the best semi-private run.

The count is of action records carrying a written summary, not headings inside a summary. Low leaves 75 levels without one. None has only button names and click locations in the action output; there is no second prose explanation hidden there.

Low finishes every game, but TN36 scores below 100. None finishes four of SK48's eight levels; its other 24 games score 100. A 100 score does not establish the fewest possible moves.

The “none” logs still show positive reasoning counters on 1,468 action records. Every one coincides with a memory-compaction item. In plain words: the system is doing recorded work while packing its past work into a shorter memory. We cannot read that work from these summaries, or isolate its contribution to the score.

Useful ideas from low

  • G50T · Level 6, move 207. Keep two helpers on their switches so another character can open the way. Max has 13 notes at this level without a useful account of that plan. Low supplies a clearer idea, but not all the controls.
  • SB26 · Level 2, move 10. Follow the smaller list inside the bigger list, then return to the bigger one. This is a teachable way to break a long sequence into parts.
  • CD82 · Level 3, moves 29 and 31. Think about the order of paint. A new mark can cover an old one. The note first proposes a base layer, then says it is not needed. The correction is useful to inspect.
  • M0R0 · Level 4, move 167. Use the top wall to line the pieces up, then move them back down. A wall can help you position a piece, rather than only block it.
  • AR25 · Level 8, move 210. Put the mirror halfway between two positions. This is a short explanation of how to choose its place.
  • LF52 · Level 6, move 267. A special peg may stay after a jump. Watch what remains, rather than assuming every peg disappears. The note is still tentative.

Low is not automatically clearer. TU93 has one vague opening note across nine levels. G50T has only three notes in its entire low run. The useful teaching fragments must be selected individually.

BP35 does not show instant understanding

The low run first guesses a platform game, then grappling. Its second move clicks a plain wall without opening a path. Later it removes a green block overhead and rises through the gap. Level 1 finishes on move 16.

There are no more written summaries between move 2 and the next level. The before-and-after lesson explains the visible changes. It does not claim that Astra wrote down that lesson, or that we know where its original idea came from.

A learner can test a familiar idea against the picture: could the ledge be holding the character down? Could a different-looking block do something different? Those are useful questions, not evidence of what Astra privately recalled.
