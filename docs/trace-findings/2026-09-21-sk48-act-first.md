# sk48 act-first — what happened

**Run:** `20260921_180906_20260921_sk48-act-first-oracle-local-mlx-r2` (primary)
**Model:** `qwen/qwen3.8-27b`, stock, LM Studio, Mac Mini M4 Pro
**Game:** `sk48-d8078629`, level 1, with the Boss's full sk48 rulebook injected every turn
**Arm:** `harnesses/sk48-act-first/` — see it for the three changes and the launch script

## The short version

Act-first worked as an intervention. Four prior runs took **zero** actions; this one took
**ten**, one per turn, and **every single one changed the board**. That converted a null result
into a legible failure, which is the entire point of the probe.

The blocker moved. It is no longer "the model will not commit a move." It is "the model cannot
read what its own move did."

## (a) Did it actually take ten actions?

Yes. Ten environment actions in ten turns, one per turn.

It needed the blunt retry to do it. On most turns the model's first probe inspected the frame
and returned without acting; the forced "press a button NOW" follow-up is what produced the
action. Three further turns failed to act even after the retry, which is why ten actions cost
thirteen turns. Left to its own pacing this model does not volunteer a move, even on a game
whose rulebook it has been handed.

Thinking was genuinely off for all ten — zero reasoning characters on every request. That is
worth stating because the obvious way to build this arm does **not** work: see the endpoint
note at the bottom.

## (b) What were they?

In order: UP, DOWN, ACTION7, RESET, LEFT, RIGHT, RIGHT, RIGHT, ACTION7, RIGHT.

A reasonable opening. It swept the direction keys, tried the two non-direction actions, then
settled into repeating RIGHT — which is the correct instinct, because RIGHT is the one that
does something legible.

## (c) Did the board change, and how?

Every action changed the board. The informative stretch is actions five through ten, where the
change is unambiguous. Watching the single board row the rod lives on (row thirty-eight, board
columns eight onward, handle first):

```
init        BBBMWMMWwgwwgwwccccccccccccccccc
 5 LEFT     BBBMWMMWwccccccccccccccccccccccc
 6 RIGHT    BBBMWMMWwgwwgwwccccccccccccccccc
 7 RIGHT    BBBMWMMWwgwwgwwgwwgwwccccccccccc
 8 RIGHT    BBBMWMMWwgwwgwwgwwgwwgwwgwwccccc
 9 ACTION7  BBBMWMMWwgwwgwwgwwgwwccccccccccc
10 RIGHT    BBBMWMMWwgwwgwwgwwgwwgwwgwwccccc
```

The mechanic is right there, and it is exactly as clean as the Boss predicted. The pink handle
never moves. The striped bar beside it **extends by one fixed segment per RIGHT press** and
retracts by one per ACTION7. Deterministic, fully visible, no randomness, same delta every
time. This is a game that can be learned by pressing buttons, and ten presses were enough to
show it.

Actions one through four produced a different and larger change in the handle region which is
not characterized here and is not needed for the verdict.

## (d) Once thinking came back at action eleven, did it read its own moves correctly?

**No, and not narrowly — it argued itself out of the right answer.**

At action eleven the act-first phase released, thinking returned, and the model spent eighteen
thousand characters of reasoning and fourteen minutes of wall clock on a single request. The
cost comparison is stark: that one thinking turn generated more tokens than the entire
ten-action playing phase before it.

What it produced with that time:

- It **correctly recited the rulebook**, including the decisive sentence: *"pressing the rod's
  own direction extends it one segment."*
- It **correctly measured its own effect**, computing that the striped bar had grown by
  eighteen columns.
- It **reached the right hypothesis on its own**: *"what I'm calling 'the rail' is actually the
  ROD."*
- Then it **threw that away** and went back to circling, because of an assumption it had formed
  at action one, before any evidence existed: that the rod points UP.

Its own words, in sequence: *"The rail is GROWING? That doesn't make sense."* … *"OH WAIT. I
think I've been misreading the board."* … *"no wait, that doesn't make sense either."* … *"I'm
going in circles."* It ended the turn still believing the rod points up, still unable to tell
the rod from the rail, and still planning around sliding a handle that never moved.

The failure is not that it lacked information. It had the rulebook, it had ten clean
observations, and it had the correct interpretation in hand. It could not let go of a
first-turn guess that the evidence contradicted.

## A confound I introduced, and why it does not rescue the verdict

Be straight about this. To stop the model batching its way past the phase (in the earlier run
it queued ten presses in one turn and jumped from action five to action fourteen), the act-first
patch caps execution at one action per turn. In r2 that cap dropped the extra actions
**silently** — the model asked for twenty and thirty RIGHT presses, got one each time, and was
never told. So when it reasoned about "twenty presses," its count came from the harness lying by
omission, not from the game.

That was my bug. The harness now reports the dropped actions explicitly in the tool result
(`stop_reason: act_first_one_action_per_turn`).

The clean re-test did **not** happen, and the reason matters: while this probe was running,
another agent rewrote `prompts.py` on main. Every run launched after that rewrite plays a
different game from a different system prompt, so it cannot replicate the primary run. Two
attempts were made and abandoned rather than passed off as confirmation.

It does not change the verdict, because none of the four failures above depend on the count: the
bar extended by exactly one segment on every press it actually made, the model itself measured
the growth, it quoted the rule that explains it, and it discarded its own correct hypothesis for
a first-turn assumption. Correcting the arithmetic does not repair any link in that chain. r3
tests the prediction rather than assuming it.

## Two things that changed underneath this probe

Both are other agents' work on the same repo, landed mid-run. Neither invalidates the result,
but both would mislead someone reading the run dirs cold.

**The a424 parser bug is a different box.** A commit landed stating that the tool-call parser is
wrong and "is why arms on this box never act" — served with the hermes parser against a model
emitting Qwen XML. That is the a424 vLLM box. This probe ran on the Mac Mini against LM Studio,
which returned native tool calls on every single request. The zero-action history on sk48 here
is not that bug, and the two must not be pooled.

**The efficiency bullet was removed on main independently.** The Boss's prompt rewrite and the
`no-score-pressure` arm deleted the same scoring bullet this arm deleted, for the same reason,
within the same hour. Change two of three converged with what main was already doing.

## Verdict

This model is not going to solve sk48, and the reason is now specific rather than mysterious.
Forcing it to play instead of measure fixed the problem everyone could see — it acts, and its
actions are sensible probes that produce clean, repeatable, fully legible evidence. What it
cannot do is revise a belief it formed before it had any evidence. It read the rulebook
correctly, it measured the board correctly, it guessed the mechanic correctly in passing, and
it still could not overwrite "the rod points up." Giving it more thinking time makes this worse,
not better: the fourteen-minute turn was where the correct hypothesis got talked to death.

So measurement was never the bottleneck, and neither is knowledge — it was handed the answer
key and still lost. More context, more rulebook, and more reasoning budget are not the lever
here. The lever is belief revision, and that is a property of the model, not of the prompt.

## Endpoint note — this will bite the next person

`thinking_override=False` **does nothing on LM Studio.** The harness encodes it as
`chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking` for provider `vllm`, and LM Studio silently ignores that
key: measured on this box, true versus false returned byte-identical reasoning text and an
identical completion-token count. The knob LM Studio honours is `reasoning_effort: "none"`,
which zeroes reasoning and leaves tool-calling intact.

Anyone porting a thinking-off arm to LM Studio must verify with a single request before
launching. Otherwise the arm never actually runs, and its null result means nothing.

## Next arm — one variable

`GAME_OVERVIEW_ADDENDUM` still warns that a value ticking up by a fixed amount per action cannot
be the objective. In sk48 the rod extends by a fixed amount per press, which makes that bullet
actively misleading on this game — the same shape of error as the HUD line the overfit arm
already removed. That is the obvious single-variable arm to run next, and it was deliberately
left alone here to keep this probe to three changes.
