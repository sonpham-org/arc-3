PUBLIC WISDOM: tricky mechanics that turn up in ARC-AGI-3 games, mostly in later levels. You are stuck, so your current model of this game is missing something. Go down this list. For each idea that could fit the board, write the cheapest probe that would prove or kill it, run the best one, and keep what the evidence supports. These are possibilities, not facts about this game.

CHECK THE BASICS FIRST
- Try every valid action once from a safe state, including SPACE, MOUSE on each distinct object and UNDO, and record exactly what each one changed. An action that "did nothing" may have changed something small, off to the side or only during its animation.
- Find the goal on the board: a target pattern, legend, reference strip, outline, empty slot or second copy of an object. If you have not found it, that is the problem to solve first.
- Compare this level with the last one you cleared. Name every new element. The new element is almost always the key.

HIDDEN STATE
- Something that decides the outcome may not be drawn: an item you carry, a count of things collected, a value set by an earlier action, or a choice made on a previous attempt. If the same move gives different results at different times, look for the hidden thing that changed.
- What you carry may get rewritten when you touch tiles: its colour, shape, size or rotation. The same tile can help or hurt depending on what you carry. Gates may check all of those attributes at once.

THE SCREEN IS NOT THE WORLD
- The board may be a window onto a larger world that scrolls as you move. If objects shift together when you move, the camera moved, not the objects. Convert to world positions before you compare frames or click.

WHO YOU CONTROL
- You may control more than one thing, and an action may switch which one your inputs move. Look for a selector, a highlight or a cursor that jumped.
- You may not be able to move the thing that matters. You move its parts, and the thing's position follows from them (a centre, an average, a sum).
- The controls may build a value instead of moving anything: switches or dials read together as a code, and the code decides what happens.
- One action can mean two different things depending on the object it is used on.

ORDER AND TIMING
- The order of things can matter as much as where they are. Items may stack in a container with access from one end only, so the last one in is the first one out, and the goal is a sequence, not a set.
- The win may be a set of conditions that must all hold at the same moment. Moving to satisfy one can break another. Plan the whole arrangement before you move, then check every condition after each move.

THE BOARD FIGHTS BACK
- Other objects may move after each of your actions, following their own rules or goals. Watch several steps to learn their rule before you plan around them.
- A finished part can come undone. Check that earlier parts are still solved after each move.

WHEN NOTHING WORKS
- Try the move you ruled out on weak evidence. Walls, borders and the budget strip are sometimes part of the puzzle.
- Try undoing to a state from before the mistake rather than restarting.
- Spend a few actions purely to learn, and say what each one will teach you before you take it.
