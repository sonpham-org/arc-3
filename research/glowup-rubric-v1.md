# Canonical game glow-up contract

This contract turns the canonical 50 into a finite quality program. A cycle samples two
active games, gives each a coherent redesign, and deliberately drives the pair apart. The
Flash corpus contributes mechanic atoms only; names, art, characters, layouts, code, and
level sequences are never copied.

## Fidelity score (100)

| Dimension | Points |
|---|---:|
| Play feel and immediate causal feedback | 14 |
| Meaningful choices and core-loop quality | 14 |
| Seven-to-twelve-level progression | 14 |
| New mechanics that compose rather than replace | 10 |
| Legibility and accessibility | 12 |
| Visual craft, motif detail, and silhouette variety | 12 |
| Animation and state continuity | 10 |
| Coherent identity and emotional register | 8 |
| Restart, recovery, and replay quality | 6 |

A game passes at 84/100, with no dimension below 60% of its available points.

Hard gates:

- Ship 7-12 real levels. Levels 2-6 each add a component, rule, constraint, or
  representation rather than merely increasing counts or reducing a budget.
- At least two late levels synthesize three or more earlier demands. No more than two
  consecutive levels may be larger instances of the same task.
- Every consequential action has anticipation or selection feedback, an event cue, and a
  settled state. Win, loss, reset, and level transitions are visually distinct.
- Critical state is encoded redundantly by shape, pattern, position, or motion, never by
  color alone. No required text, digits, cultural knowledge, flashing, hidden gestures,
  twitch timing, or motor precision.
- Animation may decorate and clarify a transition, but never hide the deterministic final
  state or become the only evidence of a rule.
- Known win and loss traces, deterministic resets, and random-policy resistance pass after
  every edit.

## Pair distance score (100)

Score each axis from 0 (same template) to 4 (strong opposites), then apply the weight.

| Axis | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Core verb | 12 |
| Controlled subject: avatar, object, field, network, or world | 8 |
| Temporal cadence and commitment model | 8 |
| Spatial topology and camera grammar | 8 |
| Information and objective structure | 8 |
| Silhouette family | 10 |
| Composition, density, and scale | 8 |
| Palette architecture | 10 |
| Texture, material, and realism | 8 |
| Motion and effects grammar | 10 |
| Emotional tone | 5 |
| HUD and feedback style | 5 |

The pair passes at 78/100. At least eight axes must score 3 or 4, including one gameplay
axis, one visual axis, and one temporal or motion axis at 4. Palette distance must cover
at least three of hue family, value structure, saturation, color count, and texture; a hue
swap does not count. If a uniformly random pair cannot clear the gameplay-distance gate
without corrupting either concept, keep the first draw and redraw the second uniformly
from the farthest 20% of eligible games.

## Diversity axes to consider before editing

Core verb; direct versus indirect control; number and type of controlled bodies; discrete
versus continuous-looking state; simultaneous versus turn-phased agency; deterministic
planning versus inference; reversible versus irreversible commitment; perfect versus
partial information; local versus global effects; topology; camera scale; screen density;
positive and negative space; silhouette language; curvature and symmetry; size hierarchy;
palette hue, value, saturation, temperature, and count; monochrome versus polychrome;
flat, stippled, striped, woven, glassy, metallic, paper, liquid, or organic material cues;
abstract versus representational art; stillness, easing, bounce, smear, trails, particles,
ripples, pulses, wipes, shutters, or dissolves; diegetic versus panel HUD; contemplative,
comic, tense, cozy, clinical, strange, or kinetic tone; forgiving recovery versus costly
commitment; short tactics versus long-horizon planning; and single-answer versus expressive
solution structure.

## Local Flash mechanic atoms worth routing into reviews

- `f018` tactile affordance discovery: unusual verbs teach themselves through response.
- `f041` single-intervention chains: one action creates a legible cascade.
- `f029` field steering: indirect control through placed local influences.
- `f033` program by demonstration: spatial action traces become reusable procedures.
- `fl007` topology-controlled mixing: partitions decide when regions mix and transform.
- `fl024` delayed signal causality: visible one-gate-per-tick propagation.
- `fl038` mobile morphing scaffold: reshape a reusable support around autonomous motion.
- `fl065` recursive scale containment: cells become navigable worlds.
- `fl067` perceptual world-model inventory: switch among views with different affordances.
- `fl075` targeted scale editing: one reversible size operator has typed consequences.

## Independent review roles

Use at least five per cycle: a first-time child or bilingual player; an older casual puzzle
player; a Flash-portal veteran; an accessibility critic; a cross-cultural art-game critic;
an abstract systems designer; an animation/VFX director; and an exploit hunter or
speedrunner. Score independently before discussion. Require median fidelity at least 4/5,
no accessibility score below 3/5, and pair-distinctness median at least 4/5. These simulated
perspectives are adversarial review aids, not substitutes for real human playtesting.
